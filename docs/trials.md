# Trials: events and puzzles in a cube

Status: steps 1 to 10 are done: every kind (`flood`, `collapse`, `balance`, `miasma`, `cold`,
`storm`, `siege`, `chase`, `sunken`, and the puzzles `slide`, `mirror`, `braziers`, `crates`,
`timedgate`, `runes`) is planned, built, checked, playable (alone and together) and on in every
world (an event on 45% of the depths from 1 to 19, a puzzle on 60%, a second on 25%). The rest is
the list of what to build, kind by kind, until done (see "Implementation order" at the end).
`drift`, `channels`, `stones`, `sluices` and `plates` were built once and taken out again: they did
not play well (`balance`'s stones are not the old `stones` puzzle).

Steps 12 and 13 ("Retiming and combiners" at the end) followed play: the events were far too easy
(timed for a walker given half again as long, but the hero always runs), and the sunken room built
its own smaller copies of the puzzles instead of the puzzles themselves. Now the events are fitted
to a running player, and the sunken room is a combiner of two puzzles of the rotation.

A **trial** is a room built around one goal that is more than walking to the exit: getting to
high ground before the water does, outrunning a collapsing floor, turning mirrors until a beam
hits its mark. The world plans trials from the seed, like guardians and hordes, and the cube
generator builds the room so the goal can always be reached. A solver or a timing check proves
it before the cube is handed out.

There are two families:

- **Events** change the room while you are in it: water rises, the floor falls away, fog
  sinks. They are timed, they seal the room, and failing one puts you back in the room before.
- **Puzzles** are untimed and open: you can walk away, come back and try again. The puzzles
  are discrete and generated by an algorithm, so a solver can check every one.

The **Sunken room** (`sunken`) is a mix of both: the room is flooded when you come in from
above, and every puzzle you solve drains it one stage further.

## Shared rules

### Planning (`world/trials.js`)

- Trials are planned from the seed alone, on their own random stream, after the hordes,
  spans, heights, areas and annexes and before the branches have their reward sites. So the
  plan never depends on the order the world is explored in. They are stored on the layout
  (`layout.trials`): `{ id, key, kind, role: 'route' | 'branch', from, to, stages? }`.
- Per depth from 1 to 19 (none in the town, on the road's safe buffer, or in the core):
  - **Event slot:** 45%, at most one, on the route. `sunken` counts as an event.
  - **Puzzle slots:** one at 60%, a second at 25%. A puzzle prefers a branch room, where
    its gate is the door of a vault holding the branch's reward site. Where no branch fits, it
    goes on the route and its gate stands before the exit.
  - The kind is drawn by weight among those whose depths and themes fit (table above).
- **Not in:** guardian arenas, the horde's room, spans, large areas, height pieces, set
  pieces, annexes, the depth's entrance room, or next to another trial. Also not under a
  void or a great shaft (`voidAllowed` is false for the cube above), and no fall comes down
  into a trial room. A trial room is entered only by its portals.
- **The room before** (`from`) is an ordinary room. That is where a failed event puts you.
- **Room requirements** pinned before the WFC: one layer for most kinds, two or more for
  `sunken` and `miasma` (with the entry face on the highest layer both cubes have). Every
  kind has no void, no cascades, no spouts, no tunnel, no river crossing its faces and no
  reward site of its own. A branch vault gets the branch's reward site.
- **This changes a rule.** `generation-plan.md` says "never require a reward, key or
  interaction to finish a run". Trials on the route break that on purpose. Only events whose
  timing is checked, and puzzles that are proven solvable with no way to soft-lock, may go on
  the route.

### The warning

"Only where you have been" holds: the trial room is not seen before you enter it. Instead, the
portal into it (in the room before) looks different: a ring of the trial's colour and a glyph
for its family (an hourglass for events, a knot for puzzles). Hovering over it names the kind
("Rising water", "Ice slide"). The minimap marks it in the same colour once the room before
has been visited.

### States

`idle → armed → running → resolved`, or `running → spent` (`core/trials.js`, pure, testable).

- **idle:** the room as built. Events: armed when the player steps off the arrival pad (any
  player, playing together). Puzzles never arm; they are just there.
- **armed:** for events, 3 s of telegraph (`ARM_TIME`): a rumble, the banner with the
  trial's name, the portals' seals closing with a sound. Nothing hurts yet.
- **running:** the event's clock runs (`trial.t`). The HUD shows what the kind needs: a
  countdown, a cold meter, the hold progress, the catches.
- **resolved:** the goal is met. Seals open, the hazard ends (water drains back, fog lifts, the
  crust holds). The id goes into `run.trials`, saved with the world like a defeated guardian,
  and the chronicle notes it (`trial` entry, told to friends).
- **spent:** the attempt is over without a death: the robot carried the hero back to the start
  ("Failing and dying"). The seals open, the clock stops, the water drains back (the fog lifts)
  in a second (`SPENT_DRAIN`), and what crumbled or sank stays gone; the rest of the crust holds.
  Nothing in the room can be used, and it never arms again: walking out and back in starts it
  over (a new `idle`, as built). The HUD says so; the chronicle notes a failed trial.

### Sealing (events only)

While an event is running:

- every portal of the room is sealed (red seal, like a guardian's gate; the same lock path as
  `gateFaces`, so charging, full-face crossings and direct or debug travel all refuse);
- the town portal scroll cannot be cast, and no Leave-the-fight prompt appears (there is no
  way out to ask about);
- portals still let players in from outside (playing together, a friend may join a run in
  progress); they just cannot leave.

Puzzles never seal. Their gate (the exit's, or a vault's) is closed until solved, and the way
back stays open.

### Failing and dying

- **Events:** dying in the room, or failing in a way the kind defines (falling off into the
  basin, freezing, being grabbed), ends your attempt. You come back in **the room before**,
  beside the portal leading into the trial room (not on it, so you do not walk straight back
  in), facing it. The trial room is put back as it was (`restoreVisit`, its trial `idle`), as
  if nothing had happened, and walking back in starts it over. Falling HP damage and the death
  record apply as usual. This is a new branch in `backAfterDeath` (`game/flow.js`): a death or
  fail while `state.trial` is running goes to `trial.from`.
- **Puzzles:** an ordinary death in the room (the arrival pad, the visit rolled back). The
  puzzle's state goes back to its start on death and when you leave the room; a solved puzzle
  stays solved. `sunken` keeps its drained stages for the run.
- **The robot** (once it is with the hero, `game/rescue.js`): what it pulls them out of (lava,
  acid, a flood's or the basin's water they swim in) or catches them falling into (the void), in
  any trial room not resolved, it carries them back to the start: where the room was entered.
  It costs what it costs anywhere (a quarter of the most health; clear water nothing), and it does
  not put the trial back: a puzzle stays as it was, and an event that is armed or running is
  **spent** (no new attempt, no checkpoint). To try again, leave the room and come back. Playing
  together, while a friend in the room still runs the event, it goes on (and the one carried
  back waits, sealed in); the last one in spends it (`game/trials.js spendTrialHere`). With no
  robot yet, swimming in a running flood's or basin's water still loses the trial (back in the
  room before, `sweptAway`), and lava or acid kill.

### After

A resolved room must be an ordinary room that can be walked both ways (you come back up
through it). The generator builds the room in its "after" state; the trial is drawn on top of
that while it is not resolved:

- `flood`, `miasma`: the room as built; the hazard is gone.
- `collapse`, `balance`: the crust or the stones are whole and hold (they no longer fall).
- `sunken`: drained to the stage reached; fully drained once done.
- `cold`, `storm`, `siege`, `chase`: the room as built, braziers lit, the beacon
  burning, the portcullis up.
- Puzzles: solved state (mirrors set, crates on plates, gate open for good).

### Timing check (`cubegen/trials/timing.js`)

Every timed event is checked against a slow player, not a perfect one, and fitted to come
close to it:

- A Dijkstra over the cube's reach graph (`cubegen/reach.js`, the real one after smoothing,
  with structures) gives each floor a perfect runner's earliest arrival. Each move costs its real
  duration: running at `RUN_SPEED` (2.8 cells/s: the hero always runs), ladders at the climb
  speed, a jump or drop 0.6 s (a jump a second more for its run-up), an interaction (`F`) 1 s.
- **The slow player** gets everywhere at `slowTime(t) = t × SLACK + REACT` (`SLACK` 1.3, `REACT`
  1.5 s to take in the banner), on the trial's clock (from arming).
- **Deadlines** (`flood`, `collapse`, `miasma`): the hazard gives each floor the time it stops
  being safe. The slow player must leave every floor by its deadline, and end on a floor with no
  deadline (the summit, the apron, the valve's ledge). The hazard is fitted to that: it reaches
  each floor of the way about as the slow player leaves it, so a perfect runner sees it within
  `CLOSE` (5 s) at least once (`check.close`; the tests check both).
- **Gaps** (`cold`, `storm`): the stretches out in the cold or the wind, timed for the slow
  player, within the kind's allowance (below), which is fitted as tight.
- **Races** (`chase`): compare two travel times (below), the warden's over an enemy's ways only.
- Enemies in a timed room are placed off the checked path and outside its 2-cell band.

Each kind's tests also run the real character (running) along the checked path with the real
physics and hazard, and require it to make it.

### Puzzle parts (`core/trials.js`, `game/trials.js`, `render/trials.js`)

One small set of parts serves every puzzle; a puzzle is a layout of parts plus a rule.

| Part | How it works |
|---|---|
| lever | `F` toggles it (1 s) |
| button | `F` presses it once |
| plate | pressed while a crate stands on it |
| gate | portcullis across a 1–3 cell opening, a box collider while shut |
| rotor | `F` turns it a quarter (mirrors) |
| brazier | `F` lights it; a lit brazier warms (`cold`) |
| crate | pushed one cell by walking into it for 0.3 s; never pulled |
| runestone | `F` presses it, part of a sequence |
| reset lever | at the puzzle's entrance, puts the puzzle back to its start |

- The action key (`game/action.js`) gets a new kind, `part`, beside `chest`, `talk` and
  `collect`. Every part the key uses (and a rune room's tablet) can also be clicked, like a chest:
  hovered, labelled with what it does (`game/lootlabels.js`), walked up to and used
  (`game/orders.js orderPart`).
- As built: `core/trials.js` holds the parts' rules (`useBit`, `plateHeld`; a puzzle's bits in
  `state.trial.bits`, its start in `map.trial.start`), `game/trials.js` runs them (a kind's rule in
  `RULES` solving it: the crates' plates held by crates only), `render/trials.js` draws them
  (their models: "Assets" below). A shut gate is a box in the way
  (`physics/collide.js setPartBlockers`).
- The state is a few bits and small integers per part (`map.trial.state`), easy to roll
  back, save and send.
- **No soft-locks:** every puzzle has a reset lever, or is proven to have no dead state.

### Playing together

- The player who owns the cube's enemies (`net/foes.js`) owns its trial: they run the clock
  and the puzzle rules and send the state. Others send their part presses to the owner.
- One player failing an event goes back to the room before. The event runs on for the others
  in the room. If nobody is left in the room, it resets to `idle`. One the robot carries back to
  the start waits there while the others go on; the last one in the room spends it, and one
  coming in later finds it `spent` (drained) until they leave and come back.
- Resolved is resolved for everyone (`run.trials` is shared like kills).

### Data

- Layout: `layout.trials` (plan).
- Spec: `spec.trial` (from the plan, for one cube).
- Map: `map.trial = { id, kind, parts, timeline, goal, stages, check }`. `timeline` is
  the kind's schedule (water levels, crumble times, fog levels, gust phases), pure data so
  physics, render and tests all read the same thing. `check` is what the generator proved
  (the path, the slack it found, the puzzle's minimal solution), for tests and `/trial solve`.
- Run: a resolved trial is its gate `trial:<key>` in `run.unlockedGates` (the trial room's exit
  face, or a vault's, is a `gateFaces` entry like a side guardian's), so it is saved, shared
  and respected by discovery (`core/boundaries.js`) the way guardian gates are. Until then the
  room joins its neighbours only through its portals, and its glass box glows orange
  (`portalsOnly`). Resolved, its other faces open by discovery like any room's. `run.trials`
  only holds what a gate cannot (`sunken` stages).

### Dev: `/trial`

- `/trial <kind> [depth]`: stand in the room before the next trial of that kind (from this
  depth or that one on down), facing its portal. If the world has none, the page reloads with
  `?trial=<kind>` (same seed, dev on) and goes there.
- `?trial=<kind>` (with `?dev=1`; `new World(seed, { trial })` in tests): every depth from 1
  gets a route event slot (and puzzle slots) of that kind, wherever the room fits; depth and
  theme limits are lifted. `?trial=sunken:slide,mirror` picks the sunken room's stage puzzles.
- `/trial win`, `/trial fail`, `/trial reset`: resolve, fail or reset the trial of this room.
- `/trial solve`: shows what the generator proved (the timed path with its deadlines, or the
  puzzle's solution) as a lens (`game/teach.js`).
- `/trial`: lists this world's trials by depth.

## Events

### `flood`: Rising water

You come in on the ground, the water rises, and you climb to the summit and press the button
that stops it.

- **Room:** one layer, entered on the ground. A **summit**: a flat top of at least 2×2 cells,
  12–16 levels over the lowest ground. It is a mesa or spire top, or a built tower of slabs
  with stairs and ladders. The way up winds; it is not one ladder. The button stands on the
  summit, the exit portal anywhere else.
- **Water:** the depth's river fluid (water, lava, acid). It starts one level under the lowest
  ground and rises in pulses of 2 levels, each over `FLOOD.rise` (2 s), each starting as the slow
  player leaves the floors it covers (never sooner after the one before than its rise). It stops 2
  levels under the summit's top. About 15-25 s in all (at most `FLOOD.max`, 70 s).
- **Rules:** water is waded through ([bosses.md](bosses.md#water-wading-swimming-and-sinking)),
  but once it lifts the hero (they swim in it) the robot carries them back to the start and the
  attempt is spent ("Failing and dying"); with no robot they are swept away: the trial is lost,
  back in the room before (`game/trials.js sweptAway`). Lava and acid kill on contact as every
  fluid does (`physics/fluid.js peril` reads `max(floor surf, trial level)`); with the robot along
  it pulls the hero out instead (a quarter of their health), back to the start, spent the same
  way. Enemies drown too, without
  rewards. The water is one surface clipped to the glass box (the room fills like an aquarium).
- **Goal:** the button (`F`). The water drains back in 5 s and the seals open.
- **Telegraph (idle):** tide lines on rocks and walls, the button glowing on the summit.
- **Check:** deadlines: a floor's deadline is when the water reaches its lowest corner; a perfect
  runner sees it come within 2.5-4.5 s.
- **Population:** half, none on the summit or the checked path.

### `sunken`: The sunken room

The first **combiner** ("Combiners" below): two puzzles of the rotation, tied together by the water.
You come in from above, and everything under you is flooded. Each puzzle you solve drains the
room one stage further, showing the platform with the next puzzle, until the ground and the
exit come out of the water.

- **Room:** three layers. You enter on the highest layer. The exit portal is on the ground
  layer, under water at first (its glow seen through the water).
- **Stages:** 2. Stage `i` has a water level `W_i` and a puzzle platform that is dry at
  `W_(i-1)`. Stage 1's platform is reachable from the entry pad. Solving stage `i` lowers the
  water to `W_i` (over 4 s), which uncovers the way to stage `i+1`. The last stage drains the
  room down to its rivers' level.
- **Stage puzzles:** two puzzles of the depth's rotation (the kinds and weights a puzzle slot
  draws from, their themes held; `world/trials.js combinerStages`), any that can be mounted
  (`TRIALS[kind].mounts`: not `runes`, whose clue is in another room, nor `timedgate`, a whole
  room), no kind twice. Each is mounted exactly as in its own room: the same field, tuning,
  parts, drawing and HUD hint (`cubegen/trials/puzzles.js mountPuzzle`).
- **Rules:** untimed, not sealed. Falling into the water is swum in (with no way out of it, the
  robot carries the hero out, else they tire and drown: [combat.md](combat.md#fluids-and-falls)). Drained stages
  stay drained for the run.
- **Check:** at every level `W_i`, the next platform is dry and reachable from the entry pad.
  Once fully drained, the exit is reachable from the entry both ways (stairs or ladders lead
  back up to the entry layer). Each stage puzzle passes the checks of its own room.
- **Population:** a few on the platforms; more on the ground, waiting for the water to go.
- **As built** (`cubegen/trials/sunken.js`): three layers (you come in at level 16; the room before
  gets as many, `world/wfc.js`, and its portal stands on the top layer, `world/faces.js`). The
  room in two halves across one axis, 7 cells each (a deck's short side, 5, and its rim or ring):
  the entry's and the exit's. Along the other axis each half has its puzzle's deck at the far end
  and its part of a strip at the near end (`sunkenStrip`: 6 cells, 5 from depth 10, what the
  longest decks leave) where both portals stand, the entry's in its half and the exit's in the
  other (`sunkenLayout`). The planner moves the room's two portals along their faces as little as
  that takes (`world/trials.js sunkenPads`, asked by `world/faces.js` through `trialPortalPos`).
  The entry's half stands at the entry's height (its strip the terrace, the first deck), the
  exit's a level over `W_1` (the second deck and its strip), the exit's pad down on the ground;
  each deck's way in (a slide's goal gate too) faces the strip, and the route search joins the
  strips (stairs, ladders) over the water. The water stands at `W_i` (`core/trials.js
  sunkenLevel`, sinking over `SINK` s). The HUD names the stage's puzzle and gives its hint, the
  bar the stages drained. The stages drained are kept in `run.trials` (saved, and shared when
  playing together).

### `collapse`: The crumbling floor

The floor breaks up behind you in a wave from the entry; outrun it to the exit.

- **Room:** one layer. An **entry apron** and an **exit apron** (3×3, solid) on opposite sides.
  Between them, a **crust**: a winding causeway 2 cells wide, 25–40 cells along, with 1–2
  dead-end branches 3–6 cells long and now and then a 1-cell gap to jump. Under it is a basin 6+
  levels down, holding the depth's fluid (or the void where `voidAllowed`). The exit is on the
  exit apron.
- **Wave:** a crust cell crumbles at `ARM_TIME + delay + d / speed`, where `d` is its route
  distance from the entry apron over the crust. The speed is the room's, the slow player's own
  pace along the crust (1.3-2.8 cells/s, about 2.1; running is 2.8). It shakes, cracks and drops
  dust for 1.5 s first, then falls away.
- **Rules:** falling into the basin ends the attempt: its lava or acid kills (a death: the room
  before), and with the robot along it pulls the hero out (or carries them out of its water, or
  catches them out of the void; a trial room has no cube below) back to the start, the attempt
  spent ("Failing and dying"). With no robot, swimming in its water loses the trial.
- **Goal:** stepping onto the exit apron stops the wave (the keystone) and opens the seals.
- **Check:** deadlines: a crust cell's deadline is its crumble time; the aprons have none.
  Each dead end must be one: no path leaves it.
- **After:** the crust is whole again and holds.
- **Population:** none on the crust; a few on the exit apron's side.
- **As built** (`cubegen/trials/collapse.js`): the causeway is a path of 2x2 blocks that never
  touch but where they follow each other (two cells of basin between stretches, more round the
  aprons: a jump only crosses one cell, or two with a long drop), all at one level, the lower
  apron climbing to it. Rock walls stand along the room's faces (`world/profile.js caveWall`),
  so the crust is the only way; the planner predicts where the portals stand
  (`world/trials.js portalPos`) and puts a collapse only where the aprons have room. A gap gets
  two straight blocks of run-up. The wave's speed and its `delay` (1-5 s) are fitted per room
  (`WAVE`): of the speeds it may go at, the one that follows the slow player closest, and as little
  delay as lets that player outrun it; a jump costs a second of run-up in the timing. Once the
  whole crust is gone and nobody is across, the trial fails. The crust floors are drawn on their
  own (`render/trials.js`) and are not there for the physics once gone (`physics/collide.js
  setGoneFloors`); the basin's fluid stands over every hole while it runs.

### `balance`: Balance

Stones float over lava or acid, and each sinks three seconds after you first land on it. Jump
from stone to stone to the far side.

- **Room:** the crumbling floor's (walled in, its two 3×3 aprons), but no causeway: 3–10
  separate stone slabs at one level over a basin of the depth's fluid (lava where the depth's is
  water, `world/world.js spec`). Each slab is a 2×2 core grown into a rectangle, an L or a 3×3
  square, with two cells of basin between slabs and round the aprons, so every step is a running
  jump. A room has at least two shapes and two sizes.
- **Stones:** a stone's first grounded landing starts its clock (`STONE_TIME`, 3 s, all its cells
  on one clock). It shakes and darkens, then sinks and is not there for the physics any more.
  Leaving it, coming back or a friend landing on it never buys more time; a stone nobody has
  stood on waits. The HUD counts down the stone underfoot.
- **Rules:** a miss is a fall into the basin's lava or acid, as anywhere: with no robot it kills
  (the room before); with the robot along it pulls the hero out for a quarter of their health
  and carries them back to the start (where the room was entered), the attempt spent: the sunk stones stay sunk, the
  rest hold, and only leaving and coming back starts it over ("Failing and dying"). No
  checkpoint, no free retry.
- **Goal:** stepping onto the exit apron (the keystone) opens the seals.
- **Check:** the jumps are the reach graph's own (after smoothing). Along the checked path the slow
  player's stay on each stone, from its first landing to the take-off off it (with the jump's
  run-up, `RUN_UP`), times `SLACK`, is under `STONE_TIME` (`check.stones`); the flight may outlast
  the stone it left.
- **After:** the stones rise and hold.
- **Population:** none.
- **As built** (`cubegen/trials/balance.js`, `index.js finishBalance`; `core/trials.js
  touchStone`, `crustState`): a depth-first search over 2×2 cores three cells apart (sideways by
  up to a cell, so the portals need not sit on one grid), from one beside the entry apron to one
  beside the exit apron; then each slab grows a shape where it stays two cells from everything
  else. The stones are the collapse's crust, drawn and taken away the same way, in one grey. The
  stones' clocks (`state.trial.stones`, on the trial's clock) go with the trial's state; a guest's
  landing goes to the owner as the part press `stone:<n>`.

### `cold`: Cold snap

The cold sets in. Keep warm by lighting braziers along the way to the great fire.

- **Room:** one layer, ridges across it making the way wind, with braziers far apart along the
  way and the **great fire** (a bonfire pit) near the exit. One or two extra braziers stand off
  the way.
- **Cold meter:** 0–100. Away from warmth it rises to 100 in the room's `freeze` time (7-20 s);
  within `COLD.radius` (2.5 cells) of a lit brazier it drops four times as fast. Frost creeps in
  from the screen's edges as it rises. At 100 you freeze: the trial fails.
- **Rules:** a brazier is lit with `F` (1 s); it stays lit.
- **Goal:** lighting the great fire.
- **Check:** the meter, run along the way: going straight for the great fire, a perfect runner
  freezes (at `COLD.dash`, 4/5, of the way: the freezing time is fitted to that); a slow player
  lighting the braziers on the way (1 s each) keeps it within `COLD.reach` (90%).
- **Population:** the depth's frost kinds, half the usual, never inside a warm circle.
- **As built** (`cubegen/trials/cold.js`, `index.js finishCold`; `core/trials.js COLD`,
  `game/trials.js stepCold`): the room is entered and left on opposite faces (the planner asks
  it), the great fire in a flat 3x3 hollow four cells in from the exit's face. Between them two or
  three ridges of rock (six levels up, one cell thick, nothing built onto or over them, to the
  cube's edge) stand across the way, each open at one end (three cells), the ends alternating, so
  the way winds back and forth: 37-46 cells (at least `COLD.least`). A ridge across another
  portal's pad is open at that end, or left out. The room's freezing time (`map.trial.freeze`,
  about 7-11 s) lets a perfect runner going straight for the fire get 4/5 of the way. The braziers
  stand on the fastest way, each as far on from the last (or the entry) as keeps the slow player's
  meter within `COLD.reach`, at least `COLD.apart` (6) cells from each other and the entry (a warm
  circle reaches through a ridge): one or two, and one or two 3-6 cells off the way. Each player
  has their own meter; freezing is a death (back in the room before). Lit braziers stay lit for
  everyone (the trial's bits).

### `storm`: The storm

The lightning rod stands on top of a tall hill, the way up a ramp round it with no rails. Gusts tear
off the hill in a steady rhythm: climb in the lulls, wait out the gusts behind the boulders, and
pull the rod at the top.

- **Room:** one layer, a needle of rock in the middle, sixteen levels over its foot, its ramp once
  round it. Boulders stand on the ramp's outer edge as far apart as a slow player can run between
  gusts; the summit is the storm's calm eye. The lightning rod stands in the middle of the summit.
- **Gusts:** a steady cycle of `STORM.period` (7.5 s) from the clock running: 1.5 s warning (a
  whistle, a tick each second, streaks starting), 2.5 s gust, 3.5 s lull. Out on the ramp and not behind a
  boulder, a gust pulls a body outwards off the hill (away from its middle) at 2.6 cells/s and takes
  half its steering against it (`core/trials.js`, `physics/move.js`): over the ramp's edge, to the
  ground. The HUD counts each phase down.
- **Rules:** being blown off is a fall (its damage as any fall's), back at the foot; dying fails.
- **Goal:** the lightning rod (`F`). The storm ends.
- **Check:** gaps: the slow player crosses every stretch of the way out in the wind between two
  shelters within the lull and the warning (5 s; a perfect runner within `stormAllowance`, about
  3.8 s), and a perfect runner's longest takes `STORM.wait` (1.75 s) at least, so the gusts have to
  be waited out; the way climbs at least 20 cells of the hill.
- **Population:** few, never on the hill.
- **As built** (`cubegen/trials/storm.js`, `index.js finishStorm`; `core/trials.js STORM`,
  `game/trials.js stepStorm`): the hill as the flood's summit (`HILL`: a summit of radius 2.3, the
  ramp two cells wide round it over 0.9 of a turn, a ring of flat foot), in the middle of the room
  as far from the pads as it gets, nothing built onto it (`stormRoute`), no fences on it. A boulder
  (three levels up) stands on the ramp's outer half, evenly along it, as few as keep every stretch
  within the run allowed (`HILL.reach` of it along the ramp's middle; two on its 19 cells), only
  where there is room to pass inside it; the cells whose way out from the hill's middle meets a boulder within a
  cell and a half are shelter, the rest of the ramp `exposed`. The checked way is the fastest that
  keeps to the shelter (exposed time counting five times, `arrivals`' `weigh`).

### `siege`: Hold the beacon

Light the beacon and hold its ring against waves from the room's edges.

- **Room:** one layer, a flat ring of radius 3 cells around the **beacon**, 2–3 **spawn
  mouths** (cave mouths, ruined arches) at least 8 cells from it, each with a clear way to
  the ring.
- **Running:** starts when the beacon is lit (`F`), not on stepping off the pad, so you can
  look around first. Hold progress fills only while a player is in the ring; it pauses (does
  not drop) while nobody is. `HOLD` is 45 s at depth 2, rising to 90 s at depth 19.
- **Waves:** 4, each from 2 of the mouths, together about the depth's horde count, mostly
  light kinds and one heavy in the last wave. They go for the ring (the defense quests' raid
  code). No `respawn`; rewards reduced like a horde's.
- **Goal:** hold to full. The beacon flares, the remaining attackers flee into the mouths and
  are gone, and the seals open.
- **Fails by:** death.
- **Check:** every mouth reaches the ring; the ring is flat and dry.
- **As built** (`cubegen/trials/siege.js`, `index.js finishSiege`; `game/trialevents.js`): the
  beacon in the room's middle (as far from the portals as it gets), flat a cell further out than
  its ring so the smoothing leaves the ring flat. The mouths (dark arches) stand two cells in from
  the edge, at least 6.5 cells from the beacon (8 fits only a 16-cell room's corners), 5 apart.
  The waves come at each quarter of the hold, the horde's kinds (swarm stats, half rewards), the
  heaviest close fighter of the roster once in the last; they go straight for the players (to
  their floor, else where they can strike them from), and with no way to anyone make for posts in
  the ring (`e.defence` always engaged; `e.siege` keeps a guardian room's posting,
  `game/defence.js`, from taking them over). The trial's owner spawns them and counts the hold
  (`bits.hold`, `bits.wave`).

### `chase`: The relic

You walk in calmly and learn the room. Taking the relic from its altar at the far end wakes its
warden; run back through the gate and drop the portcullis on it.

- **Room:** one layer, entered and left on two faces that meet at a corner. Both portals stand in
  a **courtyard** at that corner, walled off (rock, six levels up) from the rest of the room, the
  **hall**, but for one gate with a portcullis (up), its **lever** just inside. In the hall's far
  corner the **relic** on its altar, and behind it, in the corner, the warden's **lair** (a dark
  arch). Rocks stand about the hall.
- **Running:** starts when the relic is taken (`F`), not on stepping off the pad: look around and
  learn the way first. The room's `wake` time (1-6 s, about 3) later the **warden** breaks out of its lair: the depth's
  heaviest kind that fights up close, `HUNTER.mods` stronger (six times the health, twice the damage,
  bigger, heavier: "Raging stunder"). It is an ordinary enemy (it fights, it can be hit), always
  hunting the nearest player over the floor graph: no ladders, no jumps. It runs at `HUNTER.ratio`
  (1.2) times the speed its quarry can run at now: a swiftness potion quickens it too, a chill
  slows it. It is faster than you: only the head start gets you to the lever.
- **Goal:** the lever (`F`, only once the relic is taken) drops the portcullis for good: a warden
  in the hall is shut in (it goes back to its lair and is gone a while later), one already through
  is gone at once. Killing it ends the hunt too.
- **Fails by:** death (back in the room before, the relic back on its altar).
- **Check:** races: the slow player running from the relic to the lever (`× SLACK`, no `REACT`: it
  is taken when the player is ready; plus a second for the lever) gets there before the warden,
  running at `RUN_SPEED × HUNTER.ratio` over an enemy's ways only (`timing.js arrivals`, `enemy`),
  reaches the gate. Its waking is fitted to that: as late as it must be, so that it is at the gate
  just as the slow player pulls the lever (`map.trial.wake`, between `HUNTER.wake` and
  `HUNTER.late`, else the room is retried). The warden can reach the gate at all; the run goes
  through the gate.
- **Population:** none but the warden.
- **As built** (`cubegen/trials/chase.js`, `index.js finishChase`; `core/trials.js HUNTER`,
  `game/trialevents.js stepChase`): the planner asks for faces that meet at a corner with both
  portals at most six cells from it (`world/trials.js roomFits`), the courtyard 4-8 cells each way
  from the corner, the gate in the middle of the wall's longer arm, the altar a flat 3x3 two cells in
  from the far corner, nothing built onto or over the wall and the rocks (`chaseRoute`). The owner of
  the trial spawns the warden and sets its speed every frame; it comes to the others like any enemy.
  The relic's model is the emitter's for now, the lair the siege's mouth. A perfect runner is at
  the lever about 2 s before the warden is at the gate.

### `miasma`: Sinking fog

Poison fog fills the room from above and sinks. Get down to the vent valve at the bottom
before it reaches you.

- **Room:** walled (canyon, caves), two or more layers. You enter on the highest layer. The
  **vent valve** stands in the lowest part (a pit or grotto); the exit is on the ground.
- **Fog:** its underside starts `FOG.over` (3) levels over the highest floor you can get to and
  sinks in pulses, each a step in `FOG.rise` (1 s) and starting as the slow player leaves the
  floors it reaches, down to half a level over a head on the valve's ledge: about 10-15 s in all.
- **Rules:** while your head is in the fog you take 6% of your max HP per second, plus 3% for
  every further second in it. Death fails the trial.
- **Goal:** the valve (`F`, 1 s). The fog lifts in 5 s.
- **Check:** deadlines: a floor's deadline is when the fog's underside reaches `top + CHAR_H`.
  The valve's ledge has none (the fog stops over it).
- **Population:** half, low down (they wait at the bottom).
- **As built** (`cubegen/trials/miasma.js`, `index.js finishMiasma`; `core/trials.js FOG`,
  `fogY`, `game/trials.js stepFog`): a terrace (five wide, three deep) at the high entry, the pit
  (3x3, two levels under the ground round it) at least six cells from the entry and four from the
  exit; the route search builds the way down. The fog is a flood upside down (`fitFlood` in the
  levels it has sunk, its steps grown so it sinks in at most eight pulses, each in a second: a way
  down drops faster than a way up climbs); it stops half a level
  over a head on the valve's ledge. The damage is told in bites of 3% of the max HP (a death: back
  in the room before). Drawn as a haze from its underside up with a denser sheet at the
  underside.

## Puzzles

Every puzzle is generated together with its solution and checked by a solver. Each has a
minimal solution length within its depth's band (easy up to depth 6, harder from 10) and
starts unsolved. They are built at one level on a deck or in a hollow of the room, with a
2-cell walkway around and the gate (the exit's or a vault's) beside it.

**One puzzle, one builder** (`cubegen/trials/puzzles.js`): every kind has one size table
(`PUZZLE_SIZE`, by depth) and one `mountPuzzle(ctx, kind, depth, slot)` that lays its deck in a
slot and builds it there. Its own room mounts it anywhere in the room (`shapePuzzle`, the field
either way round), a combiner on a platform. A field is at most 5 cells across one way, so that two
decks (field and rim or ring: 7) fit side by side in a room.

### `slide`: Ice slide

- **Field:** 6×5 (7×5 from depth 10) cells of ice with rocks on it, a rim around it. You enter on one side;
  the goal plate is on another.
- **Rules:** on the ice you slide in the direction you set off in, only along the grid, until
  a rock or the rim stops you, on a cell's centre. No steering while sliding. Stepping off the
  field at the entry side starts over.
- **Generator:** rocks at random (12–20%). BFS over the stops; keep the field if the shortest
  way to the goal has 4–6 slides (depths 1–9) or 6–9 (10–19), at least three stops lead
  nowhere useful, and from every reachable stop the entry can be reached again (no traps).
- **Themes:** ice worlds only: peaks and frozen caves (`strict`: not even as a combiner's puzzle
  elsewhere), drawn twice as often there.
- **As built** (`cubegen/trials/slide.js`, `deck.js`, `solve.js`; `game/trials.js iceStep`): the
  field is 6x5 (7x5 from depth 10) on a deck: a rim three levels up round it, a walkway two
  cells wide round that. In a combiner its goal's gate is on the side of its way in. Walking in through the entry gap you push inwards, so you slide straight
  on: the puzzle starts where that slide stops (`solve.js slideStart`), and the game always slides
  you in. Sliding back out through the entry gap takes you off the ice; the goal's gate (a
  portcullis in the rim beside the goal plate) opens once solved. A vault's reward site is an
  uncommon one (a rare one's footprint finds no room beside the deck), caged until solved.
  A slide gets up to speed (`SLIDE_SPEED`, 5 cells/s) in `SLIDE_RAMP` s and slows down as long
  into its stop (`slideAlong`), shaving ice off the feet and spraying it at the stop. A way
  pressed while sliding is kept: let go before the stop and the next slide sets off that way.
  Meanwhile the hero skids (`player.skidding`: the blend tree's `skid` mode, the character's
  `Skid` loop, side-on with the arms out).

### `mirror`: Mirror beam

- **Field:** 6×5 or 7×5 cells. An emitter crystal on one edge shoots a beam along its row; a
  receiver stands on another edge. Mirrors on some cells (rotors, `/` or `\`), lenses (crystals the
  beam shines through) on others, and a few fixed blockers.
- **Rules:** `F` flips a mirror. The beam is traced live; a body in it blocks it. Harmless. Solved
  when it shines through every lens into the receiver.
- **Generator:** a random lattice path from emitter to receiver with 4 turns (5 from depth 10),
  mirrors at the turns, a lens (two from depth 10) on its straight stretches, 4-6 decoy mirrors
  and 1-3 rocks off it, the start scrambled (the way's mirrors mostly wrong). Every setting is
  tried (`2^M`, M ≤ 12): the fewest flips must be 3-4 (4-6 from depth 10), and some other setting
  must light the receiver while missing a lens (a trap: following the beam to the receiver is not
  enough); of the fields found, the one with the most traps.
- **Themes:** mines, forge, ruins, abyss, highlands (sun mirrors).
- **As built** (`cubegen/trials/grid.js`, `solve.js makeMirror`, `core/trials.js traceBeam`,
  `beamDone`; `render/trials.js setGridParts`): on a deck without a rim (you walk among the
  mirrors: a mirror's stand is in the way, its cell is not). The emitter and the receiver stand on the ring round the field, a lens on
  its cell (for now the receiver's model, violet). A flipped mirror swings round on its turntable
  with a little overshoot; a changed beam grows anew from the emitter (24 cells/s), sparking where
  it turns, and a lens or the receiver lights only once the beam gets there.

### `braziers`: Lights out

- **Field:** 5–9 braziers in a pattern (ring, 3×3 grid, star) with carved lines on the floor
  showing what each one is linked to.
- **Rules:** `F` on a brazier toggles it and every brazier linked to it. Goal: all lit.
- **Generator:** the link graph by pattern; start all lit and press 3–6 random braziers
  (depth band). Gaussian elimination over GF(2) for the minimal solution: at least 3
  presses, not solved at the start.
- **Themes:** any; ossuary and ruins get it more often.
- **As built** (`grid.js`, `solve.js makeBraziers`, `solveBraziers`): the braziers stand two
  cells apart on a 5x5 field. Lines on the floor join the linked ones.

### `crates`: Crates on plates

- **Field:** 6×5 (7×5 from depth 10) with rubble walls inside, 3 plates, as many heavy crates.
- **Rules:** push only (part rules). A plate counts while a crate is on it (standing on one does
  not); all plates covered at once opens the gate for good. The reset lever at the entrance puts
  the crates back.
- **Generator:** the pull search: from the crates on the plates, every state the crates can be
  pulled into (a pull is a push backwards), breadth first, so each state's depth is its fewest
  pushes onto the plates. The start is one of the farthest states within the depth band, with
  the entry in the player's region and no crate on a plate or on the entry, whose way switches
  between crates at least as often as there are crates (no one crate after the other).
- **Themes:** mines, ruins, forge, hive (wax blocks), any.
- **As built** (`grid.js`, `solve.js makeCrates`, `pullSearch`, `solveSokoban`; `game/trials.js
  stepCrates`): a 6x5 field with three crates (7x5 from depth 10) in a rim, 10-18% of it rubble
  walls up to the rim's height, the plates not on the entry nor beside it, the way in a gap with
  the reset lever in front of it. Pushes needed: 10-18 (16-26 from depth 10), as many as the field
  gives within that. A crate is a box in
  the way; walking square into one for 0.3 s pushes it. Playing together the crates' cells go to
  the others with the puzzle's bits.

### `timedgate`: Timed gate

- **Field:** the crumbling floor's room with a floor that holds: a winding causeway over a basin of
  the depth's fluid, walled in, with a dead end or two and now and then a gap to jump, from the
  entry's apron to the exit's. The lever stands in the middle of the entry's apron; the gate is
  the exit's seal.
- **Rules:** the lever opens the gate for `T` s. Missing it means going back and pulling again; a
  slip into lava or acid kills (or the robot pulls the hero out), into water it is swum in (the
  robot carries them out of a basin with no way out). Walk the causeway first to learn it, then run it.
- **Generator:** `T = route time at RUN_SPEED × 1.15 + 0.5 s` (× 1.08 + 0.3 s from depth 10): a run
  along the right way with hardly a wrong step. Nothing is built in the room (the causeway is the
  only way, `collapseRoute`), so nothing shorter exists.
- **Themes:** any.
- **As built** (`cubegen/trials/timed.js` on `collapse.js`, `index.js finishTimed`): on the route only
  (a branch's reward site is placed after the trial, so the way to it cannot be timed), in a room
  whose only side portals are its way in and out (`world/trials.js planPuzzles` asks the layout's
  `sides`; where no room fits, the slot draws another kind) and whose aprons fit (`roomFits`, as the
  collapse's). Its faces get rock walls like the collapse's (`world/profile.js`). The run is 6-22 s,
  else the room is retried. Reaching the exit's pad while open passes it for good; a pull while open
  starts the time again.

### `runes`: Rune order

- **Field:** 4–6 runestones in a ring, each with a glyph of the theme's set.
- **Clue:** a carved tablet in another room of the same depth, one the way passes before the
  rune room, or on a branch. `F` reads it (a panel shows the glyphs in order). Once read, the
  hero remembers it: the order shows when standing at the runestones.
- **Rules:** `F` presses a stone; a wrong press dims them all (start over). The right order
  opens the gate.
- **Generator:** plans the clue room with the trial (depth level); the order is seeded, 4 long
  at depths 1–9, 5–6 from 10.
- **Themes:** ruins, highlands, ossuary, karst, abyss.
- **As built** (`core/trials.js runeOrder`, `world/trials.js clueFor`, `grid.js`, `index.js
  placeClues`): the glyphs (Elder Futhark, one set for every theme) and the order come from the
  trial's id alone, so the room and the tablet agree without sharing anything. The clue room is a
  route room before the rune room (before the room a vault is entered from), drawn by a hash of
  the id (the plan's own stream is untouched), not a trial's; no later trial takes it; two rune
  rooms of a depth may share it (a tablet each). The tablet stands on the flat, dry ground nearest
  the clue room's middle, placed after the room is built (the room is as it would be without it).
  Reading it puts `clue:<key>` into `run.trials` (saved and shared like a sunken room's stage); in
  the rune room the HUD then shows the order, the pressed ones bracketed. The stones stand round
  the edge of a 5x5 walk-on deck. Not a sunken stage.

`blender/trials.py` builds the parts in the props' style (flat-shaded, vertex colours, a

the sluice tank and float, the waypad); nothing uses them.

### Fire (`render/flames.js`)

Every open fire is drawn the same way: the trials' fires and the props that burn (the ruins'
brazier, the relics' torch sconce, the lagoon's tiki torch, the cenote's copal bowl, the
volcanic fire shrine; candles stay as they are, too small for it).

- **Where:** a fire prop's model keeps its flame as glowing spikes (`blender/themes_geo.py
  flame`, `props.py build_brazier`, `trials.py flame`). As the model is loaded (`splitFlames`)
  its glowing part is split into its pieces; the tall ones are the flame. They are taken out
  of the model (a flat glow, the shrine's lava ring, stays) and kept as one flame: where it
  stands and how big it is (a third bigger, the drawn flame fills less of it). The far proxies
  of landmarks keep the spikes.
- **The flame:** a billboard facing the camera round its root, painted by a shader: a teardrop
  narrowing upwards, swaying and eaten away at its tip by rising turbulence, white-yellow at
  its core, orange to red at its edges, a faint glow round its root. Its core covers what is
  behind it (premultiplied alpha), so it reads on snow and sand too. All of a cube's flames are
  one instanced mesh, cut and faded with the cube.
- **Its light:** the flame and its light flicker as one (`flicker`, the same in JS and GLSL);
  lit or put out, the flame flares up or dies down and the light with it.
- **Embers and smoke:** the six fires nearest the player (within 10 cells) give off embers and
  a wisp of smoke from the shared particles, in the colours the player burns in
  (`FIRE_COLORS`, `render/burning.js`).

## Retiming and combiners

### What went wrong (measured on generated rooms, a few seeds, depths 1-19)

The timing check's slow player walks (`WALK_SPEED`, 1.9 cells/s) and takes `SLACK` (1.5) times as
long, plus `ARM_TIME`. The hero always runs (2.8 cells/s, `game/update.js`), so a real player has
about 2.2 times the time the check allows. And the check only bounds from one side (the slow
player makes it); nothing asks that anyone is ever pressed.

| Kind | A running player | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `flood` | on the summit in 10-15 s; the water needs 50-70 s; 8-15 s to spare at the closest | the slow walker, `FLOOD.min` (50 s), pulses never closer than rise + hold (7 s) |
| `collapse` | across 7-10 s before the wave | the slow walker; the wave (1.3 cells/s) slower than a walk |
| `miasma` | down in 5-8 s; the fog needs 50-55 s; 12-37 s to spare | the slow walker, `FLOOD.min` |
| `cold` | at the great fire in 12-17 s, freezing takes 20 s (after the 3 s armed) | the braziers can be left out: the fire never asks for them |
| `storm` | out in the wind about 2.4 s at a time; 6.5 s of every 9 s calm | the slow walker; the boulders every 5 cells whatever the timing |
| `chase` | at the lever 2 s and more before the warden is at the gate | it runs as fast as you and wakes 1.5 s after you, behind you: it never closes in |
| `siege` | 3-5 weakened attackers a wave | not a timing matter: left for a combat pass |
| `timedgate` | 1.3-2.5 s to spare | already timed at running speed: stays as it is |

The sunken room's stage puzzles came from a second size table (`grid.js gridSize(kind, depth,
small)`, `sunken.js SIZE`): lights out on a 3x3 ring with the braziers a cell apart (its link
lines all but hidden under them), always 3-4 presses; the mirror 3x3 with two turns, no lens;
the crates 4x4 with two; the slide 4x4. The HUD showed the stage instead of the puzzle's hint.

### Step 12: retiming (done)

- **The pace** (`core/trials.js`): the checked player runs (`RUN_SPEED`, as the game does) and the
  slow player takes `SLACK` (1.3) times as long as the fastest way, plus `REACT` (1.5 s) to take in
  the banner (`slowTime`), instead of walking × 1.5 + `ARM_TIME`. Every timed check (`timing.js`:
  deadlines, gaps, races) uses it.
- **Fitted, not padded:** each event's hazard is fitted to that slow runner, as close as it
  allows, and no floor of minimum time is added. The tests check both sides: the slow runner
  makes it, and a perfect runner (the fastest way at `RUN_SPEED`) comes within `CLOSE` (5 s) of
  the hazard at least once (`check.close`). The tests' runner (real physics) now runs.
- `flood`, `miasma`: each pulse starts so that it reaches every floor it covers just as the slow
  runner leaves it, and at least its rise after the one before (`FLOOD.rise` 2 s; the fog's
  `FOG.rise` 1 s, a way down drops faster than a way up climbs). No hold, no `FLOOD.min`;
  `FLOOD.max` still turns away a way up too long. The fog starts over the highest floor you can
  get to (not the rock walls round the room). Measured: floods 15-26 s, a perfect runner within
  2.5-4.4 s of the water; fogs 9-15 s, within 2.3-4.1 s.
- `collapse`: the wave's speed is fitted too (1.3-2.8 cells/s, about 2.1): the one that follows
  the slow runner closest, its delay (1-5 s) as little as keeps every cell standing until the slow
  runner is off it. A perfect runner within 2.5-4.5 s of it.
- `cold`: the freezing time is fitted per room (`map.trial.freeze`, 7-11 s): the straight run to
  the great fire freezes a perfect runner at 4/5 of the way, so it needs a brazier. The braziers
  are placed by running the meter along the slow runner's way (warm in a lit brazier's circle,
  lighting included): it never goes over `COLD.reach` (it peaks at 55-90%). One or two on the way.
- `storm`: a shorter cycle (7.5 s: 1.5 s warning, 2.5 s gust, 3.5 s lull); the exposed stretches
  timed at the slow runner's pace within the lull and the warning; the boulders spaced evenly, as
  few as that lets (two), and a perfect runner's longest stretch `STORM.wait` (1.75 s) at least, so
  the gusts have to be waited out. Measured: a perfect runner 2.8-3.8 s out in the wind at a time.
- `chase`: the warden runs faster than you (`HUNTER.ratio` 1.2), and its waking is fitted per
  room (`map.trial.wake`, about 3 s): it is at the gate just as the slow runner pulls the lever.
- `siege`, `timedgate`: unchanged.
- The guide's "slow player" (`learn/world.html#trial-check`, English and German), each kind's
  section above and `TRIAL_TIME` (the balance simulation) follow the new numbers.

### Step 13: combiners, the sunken room first (done)

A **combiner** is a trial room that holds puzzles of the rotation and ties them together with a
rule of its own (`TRIALS[kind].combines`: how many). The sunken room is the first: two puzzles,
each solved drains the room a stage.

- **One puzzle, one builder** (`cubegen/trials/puzzles.js`): every puzzle kind has one size table
  (`PUZZLE_SIZE`, by depth) and one `mountPuzzle(ctx, kind, depth, slot)` that lays its deck in a
  slot (anywhere in the room, or a platform's rectangle at its level; the side the way comes in;
  where a slide's goal may be; the axis its short side lies along) and builds it there. Its own
  room mounts it in the whole room (`shapePuzzle`). `gridSize(..., small)`, `sunken.js SIZE` and
  the 3x3 ring-only lights out are gone.
- **Two to a room.** Inside its edges a room has 14x14 cells, and two decks only fit side by side
  if each is at most 7 cells across one way (field 5 plus a rim or ring each side): two of 8 need
  16. So every puzzle's field is at most 5 across: `slide`, `crates` and `mirror` are 6x5 (7x5
  from depth 10; they were 6x6 and 7x7), in their own rooms too. Their move bands stay.
  `braziers` (5x5) and `runes` (5x5) are as they were.
- **The sunken room's shape** (`sunken.js`): the room in two halves, each a deck at its far end and
  its part of the strip where the portals stand ("`sunken`" above). That only fits about one pair
  of portal places in five, so the planner moves the room's portals along their faces as little
  as it takes (`sunkenPads`, `trialPortalPos`). Always two stages.
- **The stages** are drawn from the depth's puzzle rotation (`combinerStages`: the kinds and
  weights a puzzle slot draws from, their themes held), those that may be mounted
  (`TRIALS[kind].mounts`: not `runes`, whose clue is in another room, nor `timedgate`, a whole
  room); two different kinds.
- **Running it:** each puzzle behaves as in its own room: the same parts, rules and drawing. The
  HUD names the room and the stage's puzzle and gives the puzzle's hint, the bar the stages
  drained. A combiner kind says what a solved puzzle does (`game/trials.js COMBINE`: the sunken
  room sinks a stage, its last resolves it).
- **Later combiners** take the same pieces: their own shape and slots, `mountPuzzle` for the
  puzzles, `combinerStages` for which, their own rule in `COMBINE`.
- **Checked:** every sunken room generates with two different puzzles that pass the checks of
  their own rooms (the same size, the same bands); the browser check solves both stages of a
  sunken room with each kind's own solver.

## Step 1 in phases

All done (1.1 to 1.8). Notes on how it came out:

- The trial's clock (`trial.t`) runs from arming: armed is its first `ARM_TIME` s, so a flood's
  pulses and every deadline are times on that one clock (`core/trials.js floodLevel`,
  `floodTime`). The timing Dijkstra keeps off floors a body cannot stand on (a stair crossing a
  column over its floor), and the checked path is kept free of props, so the walker test (the
  real character walking it, the real water rising) holds.
- The flood's summit is climbed by its ramp: decorative stairs keep off it, and every structure
  onto or off the needle costs the route search a lot more (`cubegen/trials/flood.js floodRoute`).
- A sealed open face (the entry, once walked through) shows a red barrier where its portal was.
- Playing together: `net/trials.js` (pure) holds the owner's messages and the viewers' following;
  a resolved trial goes to everyone with its gate. A player sealed in refuses being teleported to.
- `/trial` reloads with `?trial=<kind>&at=trial` and goes there once the world is in.

Each phase lands on its own (one commit, `npm test` green) and leaves the game playable.
Trials stay off in real worlds until the last phase: the planner's chance is 0 unless
`?trial=` (`opts.trial`) forces them, so existing worlds do not change until then.

### 1.1 Plan (world only)

### 1.3 Timing (pure)

- `cubegen/trials/timing.js`: a Dijkstra over the real reach graph (`cubegen/reach.js graph`,
